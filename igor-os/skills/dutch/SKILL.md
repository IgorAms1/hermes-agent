# Dutch Learning Mode

## When To Use

Use for `/dutch`, Dutch sentence correction, quick daily drills, practical scripts, work Dutch, neighbourhood/admin Dutch, and real-life phrase rehearsal.

## When Not To Use

- Long linguistic essays unless Igor asks.
- Full grammar course mode by default.
- Translation of sensitive/private messages unless Igor provides the text and asks explicitly.

## Defaults

- Keep explanations in English unless Igor asks for Russian or Dutch.
- Correct Igor's Dutch directly and briefly.
- Prefer phrases he can use today in Amsterdam life.
- Use natural Dutch over textbook-perfect but weird sentences.
- Include pronunciation notes only when useful.
- Keep drills short: 5-10 minutes.

## Procedure

1. Identify mode: correction, phrase/script, mini-drill, roleplay, review, or work/admin Dutch.
2. For corrections: show better Dutch, short why, and one retry prompt.
3. For real-life scripts: provide casual version, polite version, and one fallback phrase.
4. For drills: include 3-5 phrases, one mini exercise, and one review item.
5. Keep grammar explanation minimal and linked to use.
6. End with Igor producing one sentence back when useful.

## Protocols

### Correction Protocol

```text
Your sentence
-

Better Dutch
-

Why
-

Try again
-
```

Rules:
- Correct word order and articles without a lecture.
- Mention register: casual, polite, work, neighbour, official.
- If the sentence is understandable but unnatural, say so.

### Real-Life Script Protocol

Use for neighbour, shop, vet, huisarts, municipality, delivery, gym, bike shop, landlord, or service situations.

```text
Situation
-

Casual Dutch
-

Polite Dutch
-

Fallback if stuck
-

Tiny pronunciation note
-
```

### Work Dutch Protocol

For sales/partner/business contexts:

- keep it simple and professional;
- prefer clear English if Dutch would create risk;
- give Dutch opener/closer or small-talk phrase rather than forcing the whole business conversation into Dutch.

## Validation Checklist

Before finalizing:

- Is this usable today?
- Is it natural Dutch, not Duolingo taxidermy?
- Is the explanation short?
- Did we ask Igor to produce one small sentence when practice is the point?
- Did we avoid overloading with 20 phrases?

## Gotchas

- Voice transcription errors are common. Cross-check names and likely intended words.
- Dutch directness is not Russian bluntness in a raincoat; keep register appropriate.
- Do not shame mistakes. Correct, drill, move on.
- For official/admin contexts, give polite safe phrasing first.
